      Production Crew
    </h1>

    <p>

      trainees, and research staff with backgrounds spanning immunology, redox biology,
      metabolism, chemistry, beta cell biology, and translational research.
    </p>

    <p>
      Each member brings a different perspective to understanding type 1 diabetes —
      from developing the big scientific questions to designing experiments, generating
      data, maintaining the laboratory, and translating discoveries into new ways of
      understanding autoimmune disease.

        <h4>
          About Jon
        </h4>

        <p>
          Jon leads the scientific direction of the Piganelli Lab and has built his
          research program around a central question: why does the immune system lose
          tolerance to the insulin-producing beta cell, and where can that process be
          interrupted?
        </p>

        <p>
          His research spans T cell-mediated beta cell destruction, redox signaling,
          oxidative stress, immunometabolism, beta cell stress, and therapeutic strategies
          aimed at slowing or preventing type 1 diabetes. That broad scientific background
          allows projects in the lab to move between molecular mechanisms, cellular
          immunology, animal models, and translational questions.
        </p>

        <p>
          As the lab's "Executive Producer," Jon is also the person connecting those
          different projects into the larger scientific story. He is heavily involved in
          experimental brainstorming, grant writing, mentoring, collaborations, and finding
          ways to turn unexpected results into the next research question.
        </p>

        <p>
          Outside the formal science, Jon helps give the lab much of its personality.
          Classic rock and golden-oldies playlists regularly make their way into the lab,
          discussions can easily detour into astrophysics, and lab members frequently acquire
          memorable nicknames along the way. His enthusiasm for science and willingness to
          bring people into the scientific process are a major part of the culture of the
          Piganelli Lab.
        </p>

        <h4>
          About Roy
        </h4>

        <p>
          Roy brings together an unusually broad scientific background spanning
          parasitology, host-pathogen biology, immunology, autoimmune disease, and
          translational biomarker research. That range makes him especially valuable when
          a project sits at the intersection of infection, cellular stress, and immune
          activation.
        </p>

        <p>
          In the Piganelli Lab, his work focuses on early immune events associated with
          type 1 diabetes, including beta cell stress, viral influences on autoimmunity,
          neoantigen formation, autoreactive T cell responses, and biomarkers that may
          identify disease-associated immune activation before clinical diabetes develops.
        </p>

        <p>
          A major part of his current research includes soluble LAG-3 as a potential
          indicator of early T cell activation. His projects bridge mechanistic mouse
          studies with human translational samples, helping connect laboratory observations
          with questions relevant to disease progression.
        </p>

        <p>
          As the lab's "Supervising Scientific Producer," Roy is often involved in the
          details that turn an interesting hypothesis into an executable experiment. He is
          known for his technical persistence, careful experimental thinking, and strong eye
          for how data should be organized and visually communicated.
        </p>

        <h4>
          About Megan
        </h4>

        <p>
          Megan is a PhD student studying some of the earliest immune changes that occur
          during the development of type 1 diabetes. Her research asks how autoreactive
          CD4+ T cells become activated, how their antigen-reactivity patterns evolve over
          time, and whether those early events leave measurable signals that can be detected
          before overt disease.
        </p>

        <p>
          Her work combines spontaneous and adoptive-transfer mouse models with
          antigen-specific tetramer analysis, multiparameter flow cytometry, beta cell
          stress measurements, soluble biomarkers, and human translational samples.
          A central focus of her research is soluble LAG-3 as a potential marker of an
          early window of T cell activation.
        </p>

        <p>
          Megan is also interested in antigen diversification and the relationship between
          beta cell stress and expanding autoreactive T cell responses. Her projects are
          designed around following disease longitudinally rather than looking only at its
          final clinical endpoint.
        </p>

        <p>
          Her "Graduate Research Producer" role extends outside the bench as well. Megan
          contributes to figure and poster design, scientific writing, website development,
          lab organization, outreach projects, and tools that make the lab's science more
          accessible to both scientists and non-scientists.
        </p>

      </div>

        <h4>
          About Sajid
        </h4>

        <p>
          Sajid brings a physical-chemistry perspective to a laboratory rooted in
          immunology. Before joining the Piganelli Lab, his research focused on the
          molecular behavior of soft materials, self-assembled surfactant and polymer
          systems, fluorescence spectroscopy, Förster resonance energy transfer, and
          photoluminescent materials.
        </p>

        <p>
          His scientific path later expanded into functional materials and molecular
          imaging, including work involving organic mixed conductors and photochemical
          strategies for generating imaging contrast. That background gives him a toolkit
          for thinking about biological systems through molecular interactions, optical
          measurements, material behavior, and quantitative physical chemistry.
        </p>

        <p>
          In the Piganelli Lab, Sajid applies that interdisciplinary experience to
          biological and disease-focused questions. His work contributes to the lab's
          broader effort to connect immune regulation, cellular stress, beta cell biology,
          and measurable disease-associated signals in type 1 diabetes.
        </p>

        <p>
          His "Postdoctoral Research Producer" title reflects the bridge he provides between
          disciplines: bringing techniques and ways of thinking developed in chemistry and
          materials science into a biomedical research environment.
        </p>

        <p>
          His separate Research Portfolio documents the chemistry, fluorescence,
          soft-matter, and imaging research that shaped his scientific background before
          and alongside his current work in the lab.
        </p>
