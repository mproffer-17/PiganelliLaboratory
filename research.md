<section class="research-v2-hero">
  <span class="research-v2-eyebrow">Piganelli Laboratory</span>
  <h1>Research</h1>
  <p>
    We investigate how redox biology, T cell metabolism, beta cell stress, antigen recognition,
    and early immune biomarkers shape the development of type 1 diabetes. Our research combines
    mechanistic immunology with translational models to identify earlier signals of disease and
    new opportunities for therapeutic intervention.
  </p>
</section>

        <h3>T Cell Metabolism</h3>

        <p>
          We investigate how nutrient use and metabolic reprogramming support autoreactive T cell
          activation, persistence, and effector function.
        </p>

        <p>
          By targeting glycolysis and related metabolic pathways, we aim to identify mechanisms

      >
        <h3>Beta Cell Stress</h3>

        <p>
          We examine how endoplasmic reticulum stress, oxidative stress, autophagy defects, and
          inflammatory signals alter beta cell function and immune visibility.
        </p>

        <p>
          A major focus is how stressed beta cells generate or expose altered antigens that can
          intensify autoimmune recognition.
        </p>

      >
        <h3>Early Biomarkers</h3>

        <p>
          We develop biomarkers designed to detect immune activation before overt type 1 diabetes
          and to better define dynamic windows of disease progression.
        </p>

        <p>
          Current work includes soluble LAG-3 as an early readout of T cell activation in
          self-reactivity, at-risk populations, and broader immune responses.
        </p>

        <h3>Antigen-Specific T Cells</h3>

        <p>
          We track autoreactive T cell populations to understand activation, antigen recognition,
          diversification, and tissue-specific enrichment during autoimmune progression.
        </p>

        <p>
          These studies use diabetogenic T cell models, peptide–MHC tetramers, and longitudinal

          and evaluate strategies that protect beta cells or redirect pathogenic immunity.
